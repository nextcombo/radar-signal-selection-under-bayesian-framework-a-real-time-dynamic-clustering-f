# Radar Signal Selection under Bayesian Framework: A Real-Time Dynamic Clustering Fusion Method for Radar Signal Selection[with code]

Introduction: Radar signal segregation is one of the key technologies in intelligent electromagnetic signal processing, and it is also an important link in battlefield reconnaissance and situational awareness. With the increasing density of radiation sources in modern battlefields, accurate classification and recognition of radar signals have become particularly crucial. Current RSS methods mainly include pulse repetition interval (PRI)-based methods [1], [2], [3], clustering-based methods [4], [5], [6], [7], and deep learning-based methods.
Among them, most are realized through pulse description words (PDW), including arrival time (TOA), arrival direction (DOA), radio frequency (RF), pulse width (PW), and pulse amplitude (PA). PRI-based methods use time histograms extracted from TOA for pulse separation, such as CDIF [1] and SDIF [2], while clustering and deep learning methods rely more on PDW features for classifier and model training. K-means [4], fuzzy clustering [5], [6], and density peak clustering [7] are common classifiers for PDW clustering. As for deep learning, neural networks with different structures have been widely adopted, such as recurrent neural networks (RNN) [8], convolutional neural networks (CNN) [9], and autoencoders [10].
Currently, most methods are designed for offline environments, meaning they require sufficient data for local model training beforehand. However, in real-time RSS tasks, data enters continuously, requiring algorithms to process PDW streams in real time. With the opening and closing of radar transmitters on the battlefield, the number of radar signals dynamically changes, making traditional methods difficult to handle. For example, K-means requires predefined cluster numbers and maintains them during the clustering process. Otherwise, the cluster results will be severely affected.
In recent years, incremental clustering methods have been widely applied in RSS. These methods include algorithms derived from traditional clustering techniques and stream clustering algorithms. Notably, improved incremental DBSCAN and incremental Leader algorithms have been proposed and applied to RSS tasks, enabling real-time clustering and reducing computational complexity [12]. Stream clustering algorithms such as BIRCH [13], CluStream [14], and D-Stream treat input pulse streams as different data streams. These methods typically consist of two parts: online and offline steps. The online step involves microclustering to process incoming data in real time and periodically storing statistical results, while the offline step performs macroclustering to derive the final clustering results.
However, the electromagnetic environment in modern battlefields is becoming increasingly complex, with a variety of radar signals and an imbalance of pulse types. This poses challenges because there is often a lack of prior knowledge in processing these signals. Moreover, pulse streams are dynamic, with radar transmitters frequently switching on and off, leading to unstable pulse streams. Such harsh conditions impose limitations on incremental clustering methods, such as the necessity of specific pre-cluster initialization steps and reliance on high-dimensional pulse feature vectors [16]. Additionally, modern radar transmitters are designed to operate in different modes to enhance their adaptability to interference and reconnaissance activities. The dynamic switching between radar radiation source operating modes can cause significant changes in PDW distribution for the same radar radiation source, which can lead to incorrectly increasing clusters during the clustering process, commonly referred to as over-clustering [17]. Traditional clustering methods and incremental clustering methods cannot effectively address this issue. Furthermore, existing RSS methods often rely on datasets simplified and idealized for algorithm validation, failing to meet the needs of complex electromagnetic scenarios in real-world environments.
This paper introduces a real-time RSS method based on Bayesian framework and Dynamic Cluster Merging (DCM). The proposed method consists of two main components: real-time clustering and DCM. Each type of radar signal is modeled as a set of samples from a Gaussian distribution, with Bayesian inference used to calculate the likelihood of new data points belonging to known categories. Then, the Chinese Restaurant Process (CRP) is utilized to provide prior knowledge for calculating the probability of opening new clusters. The DCM process, a dynamic measure of inter-cluster similarity, is suitable for addressing the challenge of over-clustering. The Wasserstein distance and Hausdorff distance between the probability density functions (pdfs) of classes are weighted, and the matching of PRI is considered to measure the similarity between classes. Notably, this method does not require a pre-clustering process and is resilient to pulse stream instability caused by the intermittent switching of radar transmitters.

Conclusion - For RSS tasks in complex electromagnetic environments, a real-time pulse stream sorting method is introduced. The Bayesian inference framework enables classification of signals from the same radar transmitter, and CRP is combined to provide prior knowledge based on already processed data points. A novel DCM algorithm addresses the issue of over-clustering caused by dynamic changes in radar PDW parameters. This algorithm achieves real-time clustering merger for the same radar radiation source based on similarity measures derived from Wasserstein distance and Hausdorff distance, combined with an extraction method of typical values along the line. Simulation results show that the proposed Bayesian clustering DCM outperforms traditional static clustering and streaming clustering methods in various metrics, including the number of clusters, purity, NMI, and ARI. Moreover, the DCM algorithm has significant flexibility, capable of tracking the changing number of tracked radar signals due to dynamic switches of radar radiation sources. It is worth noting that even under high pulse loss rates, the proposed algorithm still provides robust clustering results.
